National Socialist Party Ideology

Nazi ideology, as developed by the National Socialist German Workers’ Party under Adolf Hitler, was built around a set of interconnected beliefs that presented themselves as a worldview explaining Germany’s problems and offering “solutions.”

At the core was an extreme form of racial hierarchy. Nazis believed humanity was divided into “races,” which they claimed had different biological and cultural value.

Supporters of the ideology were taught that history was a struggle between races rather than classes, nations, or ideas.

Another key belief was Lebensraum (“living space”), which argued Germany required territorial expansion for resources and settlement.

Nazi ideology also rejected liberal democracy, parliamentary politics and communism.

Economically and socially, the ideology promoted a highly controlled society subordinating individual rights to the state.

After 1945, the ideology became widely condemned due to its role in war, persecution and genocide.

Key Figures in the Nazi Leadership

Adolf Hitler – Leader of Nazi Germany and head of the Nazi Party.

Joseph Goebbels – Minister of Propaganda.

Heinrich Himmler – Head of the SS.

Hermann Göring – Commander of the Luftwaffe and senior Nazi official.

Rudolf Hess – Deputy Führer until 1941.

Martin Bormann – Head of the Nazi Party Chancellery.

Albert Speer – Minister of Armaments and War Production.

Concentration Camps

Concentration camps were detention facilities established by the Nazi regime to imprison political opponents and persecuted groups.

Kristallnacht (1938)

Kristallnacht took place on 9–10 November 1938 and marked a major escalation in anti-Jewish persecution.

The Gestapo

The Gestapo was the secret state police of Nazi Germany and played a major role in suppressing opposition.

Hitler Youth (Hitlerjugend)

The Hitler Youth was a state youth organisation intended to indoctrinate young people into Nazi ideology.
