National Socialism • Information • Documentation
National Socialist Party Ideology Nazi ideology, as developed by the National Socialist German Workers’ Party under Adolf Hitler, was built around a worldview explaining Germany’s politics, race, and society. It promoted racial hierarchy, authoritarian rule, and territorial expansion. It rejected democracy and communism. This ideology contributed to dictatorship, war, and genocide. Key Figures in the Nazi Leadership Adolf Hitler – Leader of Nazi Germany and dictator of the Nazi state. He held ultimate authority over government, military, and policy decisions. Joseph Goebbels – Minister of Propaganda responsible for controlling media, shaping public opinion, and spreading Nazi ideology. Heinrich Himmler – Head of the SS and key architect of the concentration camp system and mass repression. Hermann Göring – Senior Nazi leader, commander of the Luftwaffe, and economic planner for wartime production. Rudolf Hess – Deputy Führer until 1941. He was involved in early party organisation and later flew to Britain in a solo peace mission attempt. Reinhard Heydrich – High-ranking SS leader, major organiser of the Holocaust and chair of the Wannsee Conference planning the “Final Solution”. Martin Bormann – Head of the Nazi Party Chancellery and private secretary to Hitler, controlling access to the Führer and internal party administration.
